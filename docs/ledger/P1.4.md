# P1.4 — Per-axis unique recall

**Status:** FROZEN, **revision 3**. **Base:** HOST `79989ff` on `phase-c-collab`.
**Plan reference:** `docs/PLAN.md` §3 P1.4. **Goal served:** G1, measurement only — it licenses P3.1.
**Process:** `docs/BVP.md` **Process v3** — GPT-5.6-sol first, iterating to clean with no cap, then
Grok; accepted only when both are clean on the same commit.

## The defect

`runCell` hard-codes `axis: "correctness"` (`src/review/benchmarkRunner.ts:357`). The blinded
eight-pair corpus has therefore only ever scored **one** of the three production axes. `regression`
and `adversarial` have never been measured at all, and P3.1 — "drop the weakest axis" — has no
evidence to name a weakest.

## The prerequisite the plan insists on, and why it is not a detail

`ScheduledBenchmarkCell` (`:47-53`) and `BenchmarkOracle` (`src/review/benchmark.ts:12-21`) carry no
axis, and `cellId` is derived at `:181` from
`(seed, pairId, label, provider, model, repetition)`.

Scheduling three axes under that identity produces **three cells with the same `cellId`**. Export and
rescore require one-to-one packet, oracle and response ids and throw
`benchmark packet/oracle/response cell ids differ` when the three maps disagree in size
(`:283-289`); `buildReport` builds `packetJsonl` and `oracleJsonl` one line per cell (`:416-418`), so
duplicates would silently collapse on re-import. Adding the axis to the schedule without adding it to
the identity therefore breaks the export path rather than extending it.

## Mandatory clauses

**MC1 — the axis is part of cell identity.** `axis: ReviewAxis` is added to `ScheduledBenchmarkCell`,
to `BenchmarkOracle`, and to the `cellId` derivation, which becomes
`(seed, pairId, label, provider, model, repetition, axis)`. Because `BenchmarkOracle` is what
`oracleJsonl` serialises, the axis travels into exported packets and back through rescore
reconstruction without a second mechanism.

**MC2 — all three axes are scheduled per pair.** `buildBenchmarkSchedule` iterates
`DEFAULT_REVIEW_AXES` in addition to its existing loops, so the schedule is 8 pairs × 2 labels ×
2 reviewers × 3 repetitions × **3 axes = 288 cells**, up from 96. The axis order is
`DEFAULT_REVIEW_AXES`, not an ad-hoc list, so the benchmark and production cannot disagree about what
the axes are.

**MC3 — `runCell` reviews the axis it was scheduled for.** The hard-coded `"correctness"` becomes
`scheduled.axis`. This is the one-line change the item exists for; everything else is what makes it
expressible.

**MC4 — `uniqueHits` and `tokensPerUniqueHit` are per axis, on the persisted report, for every
configured axis.** An axis that produced **no cells** — the expected shape of a partial free-tier run,
which AC9 exists for — still appears, with zero hits and `null` cost. Keying the table on the axes
*observed* lets it silently shrink to the axes that happened to complete, which is the one case the
table is most needed for. Revision 2; the entry-gate lane found `scoreAxisRecall([])` returning `{}`.
`ReviewerBenchmarkReport` (`:63-80`) gains `byAxis: Record<ReviewAxis, AxisRecall>` where
`AxisRecall` carries at least `uniqueHits: number`, `tokensPerUniqueHit: number | null`, and the
`pairIds` on which that axis was the sole finder. The daemon already persists the report as a CAS
artifact (`src/daemon/service.ts`), so no new persistence path is added.

**MC5 — the aggregation key is the plan's, stated so it is not re-derived.** A hit is unique to an
axis when, **for a given pair**, that axis produced a primary hit in **at least one** cell and no
other axis produced one in **any** cell. `tokensPerUniqueHit` is that axis's total tokens across all
its cells divided by its unique-hit count.

**Only `mutant` cells count (revision 2).** Revision 1 left this implicit and the implementation got
it wrong: it counted a primary hit in a **`clean`** cell, where there is no planted defect, so a
finding is by definition a **false positive**. An axis that only hallucinated could have been
recorded as the sole finder of a pair — and this report is what licenses P3.1's decision to drop an
axis. Found by the entry-gate lane as class (a).

**MC6 — an axis with no unique hits reports `null`, never infinity.** And `null` is ordered **worst**
in any comparison, which is the ordering P3.1 will consume. A division producing `Infinity` or `NaN`
is a defect, not a large number.

**MC7 — ties are reported, not broken.** If two axes tie on `tokensPerUniqueHit`, both are reported.
The report is evidence; the decision is P3.1's.

**MC8 — no production review behaviour changes.** `DEFAULT_REVIEW_AXES`, `reviewersPerRound`, the
dispatch loop, and the skip flag are untouched. This item measures; it does not change what a run
does. In particular it does **not** drop an axis — that is P3.1, and §1 forbids it while the quality
gate is not `pass`.

**MC9 — the existing single-axis result stays comparable, and readable from the artifact.** Revision 2
strengthens this. Widening the schedule does change `byModel`, `team` and the discrimination metrics:
they group without an axis, so they now aggregate three axes where they used to describe one. That is
a legitimate new number, but it silently redefines the fields item 42's figures were read from, and
recovering the old slice by re-filtering a persisted report is not "comparable" in any useful sense.
So each `byAxis` entry carries its **own** `QualityMetrics`, and the renderer labels the aggregate
`team (all axes)` and prints a per-axis line. The original clause follows.

**MC9b — the per-axis slice is the same statistic as the aggregate, not merely the same shape
(revision 3).** Item 42's three headline figures — 82.6% recall, 60.0% clean-site false positives,
36.8% pair discrimination — are **team** figures: `scoreTeamQuality` ORs the two reviewers over each
`(pair, label, repetition)`, and `scoreTeamDiscrimination` groups the same way. Revision 2 computed
each `byAxis` entry with `scoreQuality`, the per-cell aggregate, and printed it on the line directly
beneath `team (all axes)`. Those two numbers disagree whenever the reviewers disagree — one model
hitting and the other missing gives team recall 1/1 and per-cell 1/2 — so the report invited a
comparison between a statistic and a different statistic wearing its name, and P3.1 is what reads it.
Therefore `AxisRecall.quality` is `scoreTeamQuality` over that axis's cells, `AxisRecall.discrimination`
is `scoreTeamDiscrimination` over them, and the renderer prints the discrimination on the axis line.
Found by the entry-gate lane in round 2.

**MC9a — the original clause.** The accepted item-42 figures (82.6%
recall, 60.0% clean-site false positives, 36.8% pair discrimination) were measured on `correctness`
alone. `byModel`, `team`, and the discrimination metrics must remain computable, and a
`correctness`-only slice of the new report must be comparable to them. Widening the schedule must not
silently redefine the existing numbers.

## Acceptance criteria

**AC1 — the schedule is 288 cells with distinct ids.** For a two-reviewer schedule over the eight-pair
corpus: `schedule.length === 288`, and `new Set(schedule.map(c => c.cellId)).size === 288`. The second
half is the one that fails if MC1 is skipped, and it fails *silently* downstream rather than loudly.

**AC2 — each axis appears exactly 96 times**, and for every `(pairId, label, reviewer, repetition)`
there is exactly one cell per axis.

**AC3 — the axis reaches the reviewer.** A scripted provider records the system prompt it is given;
cells scheduled for `regression` and `adversarial` receive the reviewer protocol for **that** axis,
not `correctness`'s. Asserted against `reviewerSystemPrompt`, so a change that schedules the axis but
still reviews `correctness` fails.

**AC4 — the axis survives export and rescore.** A recorded report's `oracleJsonl` carries the axis per
line, and `rescoreReviewerBenchmark` reconstructs cells whose axis matches the original. At base this
is unrepresentable; with MC1 skipped it throws the id-mismatch error.

**AC4a — and an unusable imported axis is refused (revision 3).** Round trip is not validation.
Tampering an exported oracle's axis to a string that is not a `ReviewAxis`, and separately deleting
the field to produce the pre-P1.4 shape, must both be rejected as a schema mismatch — with the
oracle hash **recomputed**, so the check observes the schema boundary and not the hash. Revision 2
added the `ReviewAxisSchema` guard in `isOracle` with no criterion that observed it: the entry-gate
lane widened it to accept any string and all 971 checks passed.

**AC5 — unique hits, on a constructed oracle.** With a scripted schedule where, for pair P, only
`adversarial` finds the planted defect and the other two axes return `[]` across every cell:
`byAxis.adversarial.uniqueHits` counts P, and `correctness` and `regression` do not. And where **two**
axes find the same pair, **neither** counts it — that is the discriminating case for MC5's "no other
axis produced one in any cell", and a naive per-cell implementation gets it wrong.

**AC6 — `null`, not infinity.** An axis with zero unique hits reports `tokensPerUniqueHit: null`. A
check asserts `null` explicitly and also that the value is not `Infinity` and not `NaN`, because
`0/0` and `n/0` are different wrong answers.

**AC7 — `null` sorts worst.** Ordering the three axes by `tokensPerUniqueHit` puts a `null` axis last,
below any finite value however large. Asserted through whatever comparator the report exposes, so
P3.1 cannot inherit a comparator that treats `null` as zero — which would make an axis that found
nothing look cheapest.

**AC8 — the `correctness` slice is unchanged (MC9), observed on the persisted report (revision 3).**
The 288-cell report's **`byAxis.correctness` entry itself** must equal the equivalent 96-cell
`correctness`-only report's `team` and `teamDiscrimination`. Revision 2's AC8 recomputed
`scoreQuality` over two filtered cell arrays and never touched `byAxis`, so it was satisfied by a
report whose persisted slice was the wrong statistic — MC9b's defect passed it 50/50.

The fixture must be one on which the two statistics **disagree**, or the assertion is satisfied by an
implementation that computed either. So the two reviewers are asymmetric — one finds in repetitions
1–2 and the other in 3 — and the criterion additionally asserts that the per-cell aggregate over the
same slice is *not* equal to the team figure.

**AC9 — the live report is persisted and is named in the run ledger.** The run record states the seed,
the two pinned models, the cell count actually completed, and the per-axis table. If the live run is
partial — the free tier's 429 behaviour is recorded as finding 37 and finding 46 — the report says
how many cells completed and the verdict is reported over the completed set with that count stated.

**AC2a — the per-tuple property, not the per-axis total (revision 2).** For every
`(pair, label, reviewer, repetition)` tuple there is exactly one cell per axis. Equal per-axis totals
do **not** imply this: a schedule giving one reviewer two `correctness` and the other two
`adversarial` balances to 96 each and is still wrong. The entry-gate lane killed revision 1's AC2 with
exactly that mutant.

**AC5a — the boundaries of MC5 (revision 2).** Each on its own fixture: a **clean**-cell hit counts
for nothing; an axis that hit in one repetition and missed in another **does** count, because MC5 says
"at least one cell" and not "every cell"; two axes hitting the same pair in **different repetitions**
are still shared; a usage-less cell is omitted from the token total without losing its hit;
`tokensPerUniqueHit` divides by the unique-hit count, observed with **two** unique hits because one
cannot distinguish it from a hard-coded `1`.

**AC7a — the comparator's boundaries, both orientations (revision 2).** `null` sorts last whether it
is the first or the second operand; finite costs sort ascending from either position; a finite tie and
a `null`/`null` pair are ordered stably by axis name rather than dropped. A one-sided comparator
survived revision 1's single aggregate sort.

**AC10 — mutation.** Each must fail a **named** check: reverting `runCell` to `"correctness"`;
omitting the axis from `cellId`; omitting it from `BenchmarkOracle`; counting a pair for an axis when
another axis also found it; returning `Infinity` instead of `null`; ordering `null` as best. Added in
revision 2, each from an entry-gate finding: counting a **clean**-cell hit; dropping zero-cell axes;
requiring **every** same-axis cell to hit; a denominator hard-coded to `1`; a comparator wrong in the
`finite/null` orientation; a comparator returning `0` for `a < b`; a balanced schedule that violates
the per-tuple property. Added in revision 3, each from a round-2 entry-gate finding: computing a
`byAxis` entry's quality with `scoreQuality` instead of `scoreTeamQuality`; computing its
discrimination over all cells rather than the axis's own; widening `isOracle`'s axis guard to accept
any string; deleting that guard.

## The live run, and its honest bound

The implementation is verifiable offline with `ScriptedProvider`; the **measurement** needs live
models. Recorded up front: the store shows **2,600 review executions of which 18 completed** and
**zero findings**, which is finding 37's 429 storm and finding 41's transport defect — the latter
fixed after that corpus was written. A live 288-cell run on the free tier may therefore be partial,
and AC9 says what to do when it is. A partial report is a result; a fabricated one is not.

## Out of scope

- **Dropping any axis.** P3.1, and gated on P2.1.
- Changing the corpus, the repetition count, or the reviewer-selection policy.
- Re-measuring item 42's headline figures. MC9 only requires they stay comparable.

## Blocking definition

Per `docs/PLAN.md` §9 and the stopping rule plus addendum in `docs/ledger/PHASE-C.md`. A clause here
is a property of this change, not of the tree. Per the re-check lessons now in `docs/BVP.md`: a
criterion that names a member of a set does not observe the set, and an aggregate assertion over
several boundaries observes none of them individually.
